#!/bin/bash
# Gives a command the 9700X to itself: benchmark sessions and CI jobs use the machine one at a time. The runner's job
# hooks (/usr/local/libexec/buster-9700x) take the same lock, /var/lib/buster-9700x/lock, for every CI job.
#   EXCLUSIVE_OWNER=name [EXCLUSIVE_TITLE=title] [EXCLUSIVE_LINK=url] ~/buster-fast-bench/exclusive.sh COMMAND...
# EXCLUSIVE_TITLE is the session's title and EXCLUSIVE_LINK its link (its claude.ai page, or the PR or issue it works
# on): the owner file shows them under the owner's line, for whoever waits.
# Hold it for one build, test, benchmark or perf run, not for a series of them (ab.sh takes it per run), so that a CI
# job, which times out after 10 minutes, gets the machine within minutes. Sessions and CI jobs take the lock alike: none
# goes first. Calls from a command that already holds the lock (BUSTER_9700X_HELD) just run.
# How it all works: /home/david/buster-fast-bench/README-9700x.md.
dir=/var/lib/buster-9700x
status=0
if [ "$1" = "--ci" ]; then
    echo "exclusive.sh: --ci isn't needed any more: CI jobs take the lock themselves" >&2
elif [ -n "$BUSTER_9700X_HELD" ]; then
    "$@"
    status=$?
else
    exec 9>>"$dir/lock"
    if ! flock -n 9; then
        echo "exclusive.sh: waiting for the 9700X, held by: $(cat "$dir/owner" 2>/dev/null)" >&2
        flock 9
    fi
    {
        echo "$(date -Is) ${EXCLUSIVE_OWNER:-unnamed}: $*"
        if [ -n "$EXCLUSIVE_TITLE" ]; then
            echo "  title: $(printf '%s' "$EXCLUSIVE_TITLE" | tr '\n' ' ')"
        fi
        if [ -n "$EXCLUSIVE_LINK" ]; then
            echo "  link: $(printf '%s' "$EXCLUSIVE_LINK" | tr '\n' ' ')"
        fi
    } > "$dir/owner"
    BUSTER_9700X_HELD=1 "$@"
    status=$?
    : > "$dir/owner"
fi
exit $status
# The 9700X's lock

benchpress runs benchmarks for interactive agent sessions and for CI. The CI jobs come from the buster14a org's
self-hosted runner, `actions.runner.buster14a.buster-zen5-9700x`, for any of the org's repositories. To keep timings
clean, everything uses the machine one at a time by taking one lock, `/var/lib/buster-9700x/lock`.

## Sessions

- Run every build, test, benchmark and perf run through
  `EXCLUSIVE_OWNER=<name> ~/buster-fast-bench/exclusive.sh COMMAND...`, with `EXCLUSIVE_TITLE` (the session's
  title) and `EXCLUSIVE_LINK` (its claude.ai page, or the PR or issue it works on), which the owner file shows.
- Hold the lock per run, never around a series of runs. A CI job waits for the lock and times out after 10 minutes.
  Sessions and CI jobs take the lock alike, in no order of priority: `exclusive.sh` ignores the hooks' `ci-waiting`
  and `ci-done` flags.
- `x86-opt/ab.sh` takes the lock for each run itself, so call it directly, not under `exclusive.sh`. `hot.sh` and
  `hot2.sh` take it around their perf run. Calls nested under a holder (`BUSTER_9700X_HELD` set) just run.
- `cat /var/lib/buster-9700x/owner` shows who holds it: the owner's line, then its title and link. A CI job's give
  the workflow and job, the PR's number and title (or a push's first line), the run's link and the PR's (from the
  job's `GITHUB_*` variables and its event file, read with `jq`).

## CI

- The runner's job hooks take the same lock. They are set in `/var/lib/buster-github-runner/actions-runner/.env`
  (`ACTIONS_RUNNER_HOOK_JOB_STARTED` and `ACTIONS_RUNNER_HOOK_JOB_COMPLETED`), and the scripts are
  `/usr/local/libexec/buster-9700x/job-started.sh` and `job-completed.sh`.
- The started hook waits for the lock in the job's "Set up runner" step. A holder process then keeps the lock until
  the completed hook ("Complete runner") or until the job's `Runner.Worker` exits, so a cancelled job can't leave the
  lock held.
- A job's log shows `buster-9700x: waiting for the machine, held by: ...`, `this job has the machine` and
  `gave the machine back`.
- The started hook still touches `/var/lib/buster-9700x/ci-waiting` and the completed hook `ci-done`, which nothing
  reads since 2026-10-24.

## What's installed (2026-10-19)

- The group `buster-9700x`, whose members are `david` and `buster-github-runner`.
- `/var/lib/buster-9700x/` (root:buster-9700x, mode 2770), with `lock` and `owner` (mode 0660).
- The hooks in `/usr/local/libexec/buster-9700x/` (root, mode 0755).
- Two lines in the runner's `.env`. The file as it was before is `.env.before-buster-9700x`.
- The runner service reads `.env` when it starts: `sudo systemctl restart actions.runner.buster14a.buster-zen5-9700x.service`
  while no job runs (no `Runner.Worker` process).

To undo it, restore the runner's `.env` from `.env.before-buster-9700x` and restart the runner service. Then the
hooks, the lock directory and the group can be removed.

On 2026-10-22, CI went first for real: until then a session already blocked in `flock` could beat a waiting CI job,
and any session could take the lock in the 8 s from one CI job's end to the next one's hook, so each CI job waited 2 to 3.5 minutes for a
session's run. `exclusive.sh` and `job-completed.sh` changed as "Sessions" above says; their versions before are
`exclusive.sh.before-ci-first`, `/usr/local/libexec/buster-9700x/job-completed.sh.before-ci-first` and this file's
`README-9700x.md.before-ci-first`. Restoring the two scripts undoes it (no restart needed).

On 2026-10-24, CI stopped going first: `exclusive.sh` takes the lock with a plain `flock`, as sessions and the hooks
alike wait for it. The versions before are `exclusive.sh.before-no-ci-first` and `README-9700x.md.before-no-ci-first`.

## Gotcha

`pgrep -f` and `pkill -f` patterns match the command line of your own `ssh ... 'bash -c ...'`, so a loop waiting
for `pgrep -f Runner.Worker` waits forever, and `pkill -f` can kill your own session. Use `pgrep -x Runner.Worker`,
or put a letter of the pattern in brackets (`[R]unner.Worker`).
