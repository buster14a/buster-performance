# The deferred reader's post-merge rounds' lanes (#447), 2026-11-07

The 9700X's measurements for the rest of Buster issue [#447](https://github.com/buster14a/buster/issues/447). After
#1567 refitted the bodies' lane rule, it was still more than a point behind the best reading on 25 of the 76 units of
[deferred-lane-rule-447-2026-11-06](../deferred-lane-rule-447-2026-11-06/README.md). This record finds the cause. The
four post-merge rounds of #1404 (the controls, binding answers, constant contexts and expression parts) ran on every
lane the bodies took, and on mid-sized units they cost more than the lanes save. It fits and validates the change: the
rounds take the bodies' lanes only from four, and the bodies' rule returns to one lane, and one more per 3,072 tokens
and a third of the top level's beyond the critical path. Cited by Buster's `docs/c-deferred-bodies.md` ("The
post-merge rounds' lanes (#447)").

## Method

Every run is `benchmark-checker-deferred --passes P --lanes L --cpus 2,0,1,3,4,5,6,7 --raw [--every-lane] [--rss
--cold]` on one unit, as in the earlier record (`scripts/bench.sh`), each unit and configuration in a process of its
own, a batch of them in each lock hold (`scripts/batch.sh`, `scripts/queue.sh`). A configuration names its build
(`main2+every:4`). The post experiment's twelve holds ran back to back, 13 to 29 seconds each from the queue's
call; the validation's, c-testsuite's and the memory's holds ran 1 to 13 seconds each and waited up to 11 minutes
for the lock (`logs/*.queue`).

| Log | Builds | Units | Configurations | Rounds |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | ---: |
| `post.log` | `main2`, `post1` | the 76 | `every:2`, `every:3`, `every:4`, `every:8` by each | 3 |
| `validate2.log` | `main2`, `cand2` | the 76 | `rule:8` by each | 5 |
| `ctsweep2.log` | `main2`, `cand2` | c-testsuite's 220 programs | `rule:8` by each | 1 |
| `memory2.log` | `main2`, `cand2` | the earlier grid's 22 | `rss-rule:8` (`--rss --cold`, 6 passes) by each | 2 |

`scripts/post-experiment.sh` and `scripts/campaign2.sh` queued them. The campaign's first start listed the memory
units wrong and was stopped before any run (`logs/` has none of it); it ran again as recorded.

### The analysis

`scripts/summarize.c` (the earlier record's, with the phases' columns added to its runs table), `scripts/fit.c`
(unchanged) and `scripts/post.c`, C built with `clang -std=gnu17 -O2 -Wall -Wextra -Werror` (`post.c` with `-lm`):

```sh
./summarize logs/post.log > tables/runs-post.tsv
./post tables/runs-post.tsv > tables/post.txt
./summarize logs/validate2.log > tables/runs-validate2.tsv
./summarize --table pair --pair main2+rule:8,cand2+rule:8 logs/validate2.log > tables/pair-validate2.tsv
./summarize logs/ctsweep2.log > tables/runs-ctsweep2.tsv
./summarize logs/memory2.log > tables/runs-memory2.tsv
```

`tables/runs-post-main2.tsv` and `runs-post-post1.tsv` are `runs-post.tsv`'s rows of each build with the build's
prefix taken off its configurations, for `fit --units --rule 3072 3 lane` (`fit-post-*.txt`: #1567's rule on each
build's forced counts).

`post` takes, for each unit, the median over its rounds of each build's change on each forced count against one lane
in the same process; one lane runs the same code in both builds, 0 by definition. Lane counts between 4 and 8 are
interpolated. A unit's best is the least change of either build on any lane count. A policy is a bodies' rule and the
fewest lanes from which the rounds take them: a unit the rule gives L lanes reads as `main2` on L from that gate and
as `post1` on L below it. `post` scores a grid of policies by the worst unit's change against one lane, the units
within a point of their best and the gains forgone against it, summed; then prints the chosen policy's units more
than a point from their best, and every unit's medians in µs of the whole reading's, the bodies' span's, the merge's
and the phases after it's change against one lane, by build and lane count.

## Results

### The post-merge rounds (`tables/post.txt`)

On all 51 units below 40,000 tokens that take 2 lanes, the phases after the merge took 11 to 120 µs longer on 2
lanes than on one with the rounds on lanes, and 2 to 13 µs longer with the rounds on the calling thread: longer with
them on every one of the 51. On large units the rounds on lanes save more than they cost: on 4 lanes `sqlite3.c`'s phases after
the merge take 2,126 µs less than on one lane with them and 49 µs less without. At each unit's best lane count,
`sqlite3.c` reads 32.5% faster than one lane with the rounds on lanes and 27.3% without; `00204.c` 0% and 3.3%,
musl `internal/floatscan.c` 0% and 3.9%, `stdio/vfprintf.c` 0% and 3.0%, `regex/regcomp.c` 6.4% and 8.0%.

| Policy | Worst unit against one lane | Units within a point of their best | Gains forgone, summed |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| #1567's: the rounds on every lane, `max(1, excess / (3072 + top/3))` | +2.57% | 51 of 76 | 96.4 points |
| the rounds never on lanes, the grid's best rule | +0.00% | 52 | 95.8 |
| the rounds from 3 lanes, the grid's best rule, `1 + excess / (3072 + top/3)` | +0.00% | 55 | 57.6 |
| the rounds from 4 lanes, `1 + excess / (3072 + top/3)` (the change) | +0.00% | 60 | 43.5 |
| the rounds from 5 lanes, the same rule | +0.00% | 60 | 44.3 |
| the rounds from 6 lanes, the grid's best rule, `1 + excess / (2560 + top/4)` | +0.00% | 61 | 50.0 |

A term for all of the unit's tokens (the merge's pass splits them all) forgoes 36.5 points with a third constant, but
changes only synthetic units' lanes: `00204.c`, musl `regexec.c` and `vfprintf.c` still take one. It isn't taken.

### The validation (`tables/pair-validate2.tsv`)

`main2` against `cand2`, each by its rule on 8 permitted lanes against one lane in the same process, five rounds, the
two builds' processes alternating in order by round:

- Of the 23 units whose lanes change, `cand2` reads 14 more than a point faster than `main2` against one lane, 5 within
  a point and 4 more than a point slower (`b16x19.c` 4 to 5 lanes +2.35, `d16x19f384s192.c` 3 to 4 +1.21,
  `d16x19f384s288.c` 3 to 4 +2.37, `l16x38f384.c` 4 to 5 +2.46 points; they read 8.5 to 20.3% faster than one lane).
  Summed over the 23, `cand2` gains 44.23 points.
- Among them: musl `regcomp.c` 2 to 3 lanes, −0.82 to −7.49% (−6.55 points); musl `floatscan.c` 1 to 2, −0.01 to
  −3.02%; `h16x19.c` 2 to 3, +2.86 to −3.63%; `l16x10f384.c` 1 to 2, +0.20 to −5.49%.
- The 53 units both read on the same lanes came within −0.78 to +1.98 points of each other (`sqlite3.c` on 8, −32.76
  and −32.38%).
- The worst unit against one lane: `main2` `h16x19.c`, +2.86%; `cand2` `00001.c`, a program of 9 tokens both read on
  one lane, +1.00%.

### c-testsuite and the memory

Both builds read all 220 of c-testsuite's programs on one lane (`tables/runs-ctsweep2.tsv`). With `--rss --cold`,
each reading's growth over its start is alike in both builds but where a unit takes one lane more: `regcomp.c`'s
17.5 MiB on 3 lanes against 17.4 on 2, `floatscan.c`'s 13.2 MiB on 2 against 12.8 on one (`tables/runs-memory2.tsv`).

### The gaps left

Against each unit's best of either build at any lane count, 16 units stay more than a point behind (`post.txt`),
where #1567's policy left 25. The real units among them: `00204.c` (3.25 points), musl `regex/regexec.c` (3.35) and
`stdio/vfprintf.c` (3.03), which 2 lanes without the rounds read about 3% faster than one lane, and musl
`thread/pthread_create.c`, `malloc/mallocng/malloc.c` and `locale/iconv.c` (1.15 to 1.29). The largest, 3.59 to 4.04
points, are synthetic: two equal bodies of 16,645 and of 24,965 tokens on one lane, and one of 9,989 beside fifteen of
993 on 3 lanes, whose best is 4 lanes without the rounds, which no gate on the bodies' lanes chooses.

## Limitations

- The experiment's 76 units have three rounds; the validation's five. Readings of the same work in two builds of the
  same code differ by up to about 2 points in a round's median.
- Lane counts between 4 and 8 are interpolated in the scoring; the validation measures the rules' own lanes.
- A unit's best counts readings no gate on the bodies' lanes can choose (4 lanes without the rounds, 2 with them).
- The C driver reads a unit apart only where its bodies take 4 lanes or more (`BUSTER_CHECK_DEFERRED_COMPILE_LANES`),
  where the rounds take the lanes as before; its rule's count changes with the bodies' rule.
