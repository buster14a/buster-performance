# Tier 1 allocation: paired capacity-boundary sweep (buster PR #941)

Evidence for [buster PR #941](https://github.com/buster14a/buster/pull/941), the #140 stopgap. This is the old/new
capacity-boundary execution that [the stopgap contract](https://github.com/buster14a/buster/issues/140#issuecomment-6534648703)
adopted and [the latest review](https://github.com/buster14a/buster/pull/941#pullrequestreview-5722130107) left open
as a landing gate.

The change stops `tier1_allocate`'s vector path from pushing four arena columns that the old coloring used: parents,
next, deltas and pressure, 24 bytes per interval. It changes nothing that `tier1_allocate_scalar` pushes. At one
capacity, the new vector path may therefore succeed where the old one runs out of room. This record finds every
case's boundary in both builds, and checks how each call fails below it.

## What the driver checks

`scripts/capacity.c` runs each case through both production entry points:

- once with 64 MiB of room, the ample call;
- then at capacities from the arena's 19-byte prefix up. A case whose boundary is within 32 KiB of the prefix takes
  every capacity, up to 256 bytes past its boundary. A larger one takes a binary search for its boundary, every
  capacity within 64 bytes of it, the first 256 capacities, the powers of two below it with their neighbours, and 256
  evenly spaced capacities.

Every call starts from 19 known prefix bytes, a result object filled with a pattern, and 64 known bytes past its
capacity. After each call:

- the prefix and the 64 bytes past the capacity are unchanged, and so are the arena's base and capacity;
- a success has the ample call's outputs: a hash of every public field, and the retained and scratch bytes. Its arena
  bytes were poisoned with another pattern than the ample call's, and the arena holds the prefix and the retained
  bytes;
- a failure is `TIER1_ALLOCATION_CAPACITY` or the ample call's own error. The arena is back at the prefix, and the
  whole result object is unchanged, byte for byte;
- the statuses settle: every call below the boundary is `CAPACITY`, and every call from it on gives the ample call's
  status. The capacity just below the boundary was called;
- the boundary is the ample call's peak, the most arena bytes its pushes took.

Its cases:

- the #168 differential's 3,210 cases, generated by the same code from the same seeds, so they keep that record's
  numbers;
- `tier1_allocate_expiry_test.c`'s four literal fixtures and its impossible input, in both classes;
- a valid base of six intervals, and 17 inputs that change it once each:
  - 14 that the input validation refuses;
  - 2 refused after the arena's pushes, where one value's intervals overlap or change class;
  - 1 impossible constraint.

`scripts/compare.c` pairs the two builds' lines and fails unless:

- every ample outcome is the same in both builds;
- no boundary is higher in head;
- every difference is the removed columns'. For a call that gets past the input validation, `tier1_allocate`'s
  boundary and a success's scratch bytes are 24 bytes per interval lower in head. Every column and every push after
  them is 4-byte aligned. `tier1_allocate_scalar`'s are the same.

- **Ample outcomes** are the same in both builds for every case and entry point. Every status and hash of #168's
  3,210 cases is also #168's 9700X differential's, in all 6,420 runs. Each of the 6,204 successes retains 1 byte more:
  the first push's alignment after this driver's 19-byte prefix.
- **`tier1_allocate_scalar`** has every boundary the same in both builds.
- **`tier1_allocate`'s boundary is lower in head** in 3,070 of the 3,111 successes, by exactly 24 bytes per
  interval. The other 41 have no intervals. Each capacity in between is one where main returns `CAPACITY` and head
  succeeds: 43,437,576 such capacities, the improvement the contract expects. No boundary is higher in head.
- **Refusals:**
  - The 14 inputs that the validation refuses give `INVALID` at every capacity, even with no room, in both builds.
    The validation comes before any push.
  - The two refused after the pushes give `CAPACITY` until the pushes fit, then `INVALID`.
  - The 111 impossible constraints give `CAPACITY` until their pushes fit, then `CONSTRAINT`. They are #168's 108
    refusals, the expiry test's impossible input in both classes, and the malformed one.

  Both builds keep that order; head only moves the vector path's switch down by 24 bytes per interval. The allocation
  guide says that errors restore the arena's entry length and keep the result object. It also says a capacity failure
  is distinct from invalid input and from an impossible constraint. It orders them no further.
- **Every failure kept its state.** All 58,937,871 `CAPACITY` failures and every refused call left these as they were:
  - the arena's length at the prefix;
  - the prefix's 19 bytes, and the 64 bytes past the capacity;
  - the result object, byte for byte.
- **The expiry fixtures' layout** (`fixture-layout-*`): the vector path's boundary is 416 bytes in main and 320 in
  head, and the scalar path's 288 in both. That's the layout the reviewers derived, now executed.

### Mutants

`scripts/mutants.sh` builds the driver against four copies of the head tree, each with `tier1_allocate_impl` changed
in one place (`logs/7940hs/mutate-*.log`). The comparison with main fails for all four (`logs/7940hs/mutant-*.log`).
The violation counts are the driver's totals; its log prints the first four of each case:

| Mutant | Change | What the sweep reports |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `keep-arena` | a failure doesn't pop the arena back to its entry length | 27,409,981 violations: a failure's arena length isn't the prefix |
| `write-result` | a failure writes the result object | 27,829,571 violations: a failure changed the result object |
| `extra-scratch` | the vector path pushes 8 more scratch bytes that it never uses | no violation in the driver; the comparison finds 3,224 differences the removed columns don't explain, and 41 boundaries higher in head |
| `unzeroed-values` | the value checks' column isn't cleared, so the checks read the arena's earlier bytes | 31,911 violations: successes where the ample call failed, statuses that don't settle, unexpected errors |

## Limitations

- **Sampled sweeps:** 205 cases per entry point have their boundaries more than 32 KiB past the prefix: #168's 2,048-
  to 16,384-interval workloads and its large random inputs, up to 3,217,632 bytes. They take sampled capacities rather
  than every one. A sampled sweep could miss a range of pushes narrower than its spacing. The same pushes happen in
  the small cases, which take every capacity.
- **One placement:** one prefix length, 19 bytes, and one base alignment, 64 bytes.
- **Hashed outputs:** outputs are compared by a 64-bit FNV-1a hash of every public field, as in #168. A collision
  would hide a difference.
- **Nearby writes only:** only the 64 bytes past the capacity are checked for writes.
- **Uninitialized reads:** poisoning shows that the successes don't depend on the arena's earlier bytes under these
  patterns. It can't prove that no read of uninitialized bytes exists.
