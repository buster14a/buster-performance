# #1668 and #1667: a dominant body's unit in source order, and the walk's estimate again

Two follow-ups of #447, measured on the 9700X on 2026-11-22 and 23:

- **#1668:** whether the C driver should read a unit in source order where one or two dominant bodies leave the
  deferred reader's lanes too little to share, by the rule's excess (the bodies' tokens beyond the critical path, the
  larger of the largest body and twice the second) over the unit's tokens, below half; validated on shapes not used to
  choose the threshold, and end to end.
- **#1667:** whether the 2-lane gap of [deferred-gap-447-2026-11-07](../deferred-gap-447-2026-11-07/README.md) still
  exists at main, and whether an estimate of the bodies' walk time from their tokens' kinds predicts it better than token
  counts on held-out units.

Cited by Buster's `docs/c-deferred-bodies.md` ("A dominant body (#1668)" and "The walk's estimate, again (#1667)").

## Method

- **Readings (`logs/survey.log`, `logs/dom.log`):** `benchmark-checker-deferred --raw --cpus 2,0,1,3,4,5,6,7` (the
  command on CPU 2, the lanes' team on the list), 24 passes (12 for `sqlite3.c`), each pass source order's reader, the
  deferred reader on one lane and on the configuration's lanes in one of six orders. `rule:8` is the rule on 8
  permitted lanes; `every:2` is `--every-lane --lanes 2`. Each process's ratios are the medians over its passes of
  each pass's ratio; the tables give the median over processes and their range. #1667's 15 units in four processes
  (rounds 1, 2, 3 and 5), #1668's 21 in four (rounds 1, 3, 4 and 5): round 2 of `dom` and round 4 of `survey` were
  lost to the host's full disk (`/home` at 100% on 2026-11-22, its logs' appends failed), and round 1 of `survey`'s
  first two groups ran on a tree that didn't exist (exit 127, ignored).
- **Holds:** every run in a hold of `exclusive.sh` of its own (`scripts/queue.sh`, `scripts/rest.sh`); a hold of the
  survey's or dom's units lasted 3 to 15 seconds, and the lock's queue 5 to 12 minutes, so the runs were merged into
  fewer holds after the first round (`scripts/survey2.sh`, then `scripts/rest.sh`).
- **Whole drivers (`logs/e2e.log`):** `time-runs --runs 11 --build main --build cand`, a fresh process per run in a
  Williams design's order, `-c -w` with `--deferred-bodies` (list 1) and without (list 2, source order) on each of
  #1668's 21 units, unpinned (every CPU, as a compile runs: `ab --cpu N` would pin the process to one CPU and the
  reader would read every body in source order), three rounds, each round's units in one hold (`scripts/e2e.sh`); round 2's output was lost to the full disk.
  `-fsyntax-only` reads every unit in source order, so it isn't measured. Wall time is fork to `wait4`; CPU time is
  user plus system.
- **The probe (`logs/probe.tgz`):** the probe build's `benchmark-checker-deferred --lanes 1 --passes 6` on every musl,
  c-testsuite and Lua unit and `sqlite3.c` (1,752 units with bodies), in one hold; a body's walk is the median of its
  12 one-lane readings. `analysis/fit2.py` fits each model by non-negative least squares on the training units' bodies
  (each weighted by 1/sqrt(walk)) and scores each unit's whole walk on training and held-out units (Lua's, and a third
  of musl's and c-testsuite's by a hash of the name).

**The estimate.** Units' whole one-lane walks predicted from their bodies, scored by |log(predicted/measured)|
(`tables/fit.txt`): `tokens` is a cost per body and per token; `groups` a cost per body and per token of eight groups of
parser kinds (identifiers; literals; parentheses and brackets; braces, semicolons, colons and commas; `.` and `->`;
declaration keywords; control keywords; the operators).

| Units scored | Model | Training: median, 90th percentile | Held out: median, 90th percentile |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| 2,000 body tokens or more (334 training, 182 held out) | tokens | 6.3%, 22.9% | 6.3%, 41.3% |
| | groups | 14.7%, 21.9% | 14.4%, 26.9% |
| 4,000 or more (7, 18) | tokens | 41.3%, 78.8% | 19.8%, 63.1% |
| | groups | 15.9%, 50.2% | 14.0%, 31.6% |

The groups' fit keeps two of the eight: 85.5 ns per brace, semicolon, colon or comma and 49.5 ns per declaration
keyword. It is better than token counts on the 18 large held-out units and in the tails, and worse at the median of the
mid-sized ones, of which musl's share many identical inline bodies from its headers. A token's walk time also grows
with the unit (8.8 ns in `lvm.c`, 20.7 ns in `sqlite3.c`, 23.6 in `00204.c`), which neither model sees. No unit the
estimate would move reaches the driver's four lanes, so #1667's rule change would change no compile; it isn't made.
